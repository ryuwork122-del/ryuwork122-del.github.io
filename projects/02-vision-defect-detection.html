Vision Defect Detection

A convolutional vision model that flags manufacturing defects from a conveyor camera feed, tuned for high recall so nothing questionable ships unchecked.

RoleML engineering
Year2026
TypeApplied research
StackPyTorch · OpenCV · ONNX

The problem

Inspection on the line was manual and inconsistent. Two people looking at the same part disagreed often enough that reject decisions were hard to defend, and anything missed reached a customer. The brief was to catch defects automatically — but the real constraint was asymmetric: a false positive costs a few seconds of review, while a false negative costs a shipment.

Approach

I started with the data, not the architecture. Frames were sampled from the line camera, cropped to the region of interest, and labelled into defect classes plus a clean class. Lighting variance was the dominant nuisance variable, so augmentation focused there — brightness, contrast and exposure shifts — rather than on the geometric flips that would not occur in a fixed camera position.

The model is a ResNet-style CNN with a pretrained backbone, fine-tuned in two stages: the head first against a frozen backbone, then the last block unfrozen at a much lower learning rate. Class weighting compensated for the natural imbalance, since defects are rare by definition.

Because recall mattered more than precision, the decision threshold was tuned on a held-out set to hit a recall target, and I reported the full precision–recall curve rather than a single accuracy number that would have flattered the result.

What was hard

Splitting the data honestly. Frames from the same production run are highly correlated, so a random train/test split leaks near-duplicates across the boundary and inflates every metric. Grouping by run and splitting by group dropped the reported score by several points and made it real.

The second problem was latency. The model had to keep up with the line, so I exported to ONNX and benchmarked inference on the deployment hardware — which meant validating that the exported graph produced numerically equivalent outputs to the training path before trusting it.

Outcome

The detector runs against the live feed and routes flagged frames to a review queue with the crop and a confidence score attached. Reviewers now confirm or dismiss a suggestion instead of scanning every part, and every dismissal is logged — which became the feedback loop for the next round of training data.

Takeaways

Pick the metric that matches the cost of being wrong, then split your data so that metric is honest. Everything else about a vision project is downstream of those two decisions.
