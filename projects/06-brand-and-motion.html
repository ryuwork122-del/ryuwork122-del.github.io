Brand System & Motion Reel

A full identity — logo, type scale, colour system and component library in Figma — plus a launch video cut and graded in DaVinci Resolve.

The brief

A young company with a good product and no consistent visual language. Every deck, page and social post looked like it came from a different business, because it did — whoever made it last picked their own colours and fonts. They needed a system, not just a logo, and they needed to launch with it.

Approach

I started with the strategy question before opening a design tool: who is this for, what should it feel like, and what should it never feel like. The answers set constraints that made the visual decisions much faster — confident but not aggressive, technical but not cold.

The mark is built on a geometric grid so it stays crisp from a 16-pixel favicon to a conference banner, with a primary lockup, a stacked variant and a symbol-only form. From there I built the surrounding system: a type scale with defined sizes and line heights, a colour palette with accessible contrast pairs and semantic tokens for surface, text and state, and spacing rules on a 4-pixel base.

Everything lives in a Figma library as components and variables, so the team composes layouts from approved parts instead of re-inventing them. I documented usage — when to use which variant, what the minimum clear space is, which colour combinations pass contrast — because a system nobody understands gets abandoned in a month.

The launch film was shot and assembled to that same system: DaVinci Resolve for the edit, colour grade and delivery, with motion graphics built to the brand's type and easing rules so the video and the product feel like one thing. Delivered as a 60-second hero cut plus 15- and 6-second social edits from the same timeline.

What was hard

Restraint. The first colour explorations were too loud to put behind text, and the fix was not to soften them but to separate roles: an accent for emphasis and a quieter field for backgrounds. Building dark and light variants of the whole palette at the start — rather than after the fact — saved a painful retrofit.

Delivery formats came second. One master grade looked wrong on social where the saturation and contrast get re-encoded, so the social cuts have their own grade and loudness targets rather than a straight export.

Outcome

The team now ships decks, pages and posts that clearly belong together, and a new hire can produce on-brand work in an afternoon using the library. The launch film and its social edits carried the identity into the campaign instead of contradicting it.

Takeaways

A brand is the rules, not the logo. If the system is documented, componentised and easy to follow, it survives contact with the people who have to use it every day.
